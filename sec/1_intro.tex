\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Subject-to-video (S2V) generation aims to synthesize a video that depicts a user-specified subject, given a reference image of that subject and a text prompt. The task sits at the intersection of two capabilities that modern diffusion models have acquired separately: faithful subject preservation, which has matured in the image domain \cite{ruiz2023dreambooth, ye2023ipadapter, tan2024ominicontrol, wu2025uno}, and temporally coherent motion synthesis, which is now provided by large pretrained text-to-video models \cite{wan2025, kong2024hunyuanvideo}. Recent S2V systems \cite{liu2025phantom, jiang2025vace, fei2025skyreelsa2, hu2025hunyuancustom, deng2025magref, xue2025standin} bring the two together by fine-tuning a video backbone on paired reference--video data.

This recipe is limited by its data. First, paired reference--video data are scarce and expensive to collect: every training clip must be annotated with a clean, subject-centric reference whose identity is verified across the clip. Second, and more fundamentally, the pairs that \emph{can} be collected at scale are biased in a way that harms the learned behavior. In practice the reference is cropped from a frame of the same video, so it shares pose, viewpoint, lighting, and even background with the target. A model trained on such pairs is rewarded for a trivial solution: paste the reference into the frame. This \emph{copy-paste} behavior has been documented by recent benchmarks \cite{yuan2025opens2v, muss2025} and manifests as subjects that are frozen in the reference pose, fail to follow prompted actions, and transfer reference lighting and background into the generated scene.

Image data do not suffer from either problem to the same degree. Subject-driven \emph{image} datasets are orders of magnitude larger \cite{xiao2024omnigen, tan2024ominicontrol, wu2025uno}, and their reference--target pairs are constructed to vary: the same subject appears in a different pose, scene, viewpoint, or with altered attributes. A model trained on them must preserve identity while \emph{re-rendering} the subject, rather than copying it. This leads to the central proposition of this paper: \textbf{appearance should be learned from images, and the video model should contribute only motion.}

Realizing this proposition is not straightforward, because images carry no temporal information. If a video model is naively supervised with images (for example, by repeating an image into a static clip), the model learns that the correct output is a still video, and the motion prior of the backbone is destroyed in the process. Any image-only training scheme for S2V therefore has to satisfy three requirements simultaneously: (i) motion must be \emph{borrowed} from the pretrained video model, since it cannot be learned from images; (ii) appearance must be learned from image pairs; and (iii) the two must be \emph{decoupled}, so that static image supervision shapes appearance without overwriting motion, and motion can be recovered freely at inference time.

We propose \method, a two-stage framework built on a frozen video diffusion transformer that meets these requirements by construction. Stage I turns the frozen backbone into a \emph{controllable motion carrier}: a low-rank adapter on the spatiotemporal self-attention, in the spirit of Still-Moving \cite{chefer2024stillmoving}, is trained so that a runtime scale $M$ interpolates between a static regime ($M{=}1$), the native motion of the backbone ($M{=}0$), and amplified motion ($M{<}0$). Stage II learns appearance from image pairs through a reference branch that injects reference tokens into the backbone's self-attention, following Stand-In \cite{xue2025standin}. The key to decoupling is twofold. During Stage II training the motion carrier is run at $M{=}1$, so the carrier itself already produces the static video that the image supervision demands; the reference branch is therefore never asked to suppress motion. In addition, the reference contribution is read out as a \emph{paired difference}: for every input, the reference branch is evaluated once with the real reference and once with a learned null reference, and only the difference between the two predictions is added to the carrier's output. Everything that does not depend on the reference cancels, and the motion carrier is never touched by reference-dependent computation within a forward pass. At inference, $M$ is set to zero or negative to restore motion, and text and reference guidance are applied as independent terms.

Our contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
  \item We identify copy-paste as a consequence of how paired video data are constructed, and propose to train subject-to-video generation from image pairs alone, using the pretrained video model purely as a source of motion.
  \item We introduce a decoupled architecture in which a controllable motion carrier and an image-trained reference branch share a frozen backbone but maintain independent hidden states, and in which the reference signal is extracted through a paired real-versus-null difference readout.
  \item We show that running the motion carrier in its static regime during training lets a video model absorb static image supervision without losing its motion prior, and that motion, text adherence, and reference adherence become three independently adjustable controls at inference time.
\end{itemize}
